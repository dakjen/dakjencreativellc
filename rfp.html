The Problem

Firms like yours don't lose contracts because the work isn't there. They lose them to bandwidth.

The solicitation posts on a Tuesday. Someone means to look at it. By the time anyone opens the PDF it is Thursday of the following week, the questions deadline has passed, and what goes in is a rushed version of what could have gone in.

This is not a scrambling problem, and it is not a capability problem. It is what happens when the people qualified to write the proposal are the same people delivering the work that pays this month's bills.

The pipeline is the part nobody owns. That is the part we own.

Fit

Who this is for — and who it isn't.

This is for you if

You hold certifications — MBE, WBE, WOSB, DBE, 8(a), SDVOSB — and know they should be generating more pursuits than they currently are.

You have delivered the kind of work public and institutional buyers procure, and you can point to it.

You have capacity to take on a contract if you won one, and the bandwidth problem is the pipeline in front of it, not the delivery behind it.

This is not for you if

You are looking for someone to submit on your behalf, or to sign anything. We draft; you submit.

You need a win this month. Procurement cycles run on their own calendar, and anyone who promises otherwise is selling you something else.

Sectors, Stated Plainly

We pursue work across sectors. Community development, education, housing, economic development, arts and culture, nonprofits, public interest — and plenty outside that list. If an agency buys it and your firm can deliver it, it belongs in the pipeline.

The fit filter is about you, not the sector: your certifications, your NAICS codes, your geography, and what you are actually positioned to win. That is what gets scored.
